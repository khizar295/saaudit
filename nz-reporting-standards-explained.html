NZ Reporting Standards Explained

10 min read Published 2025 Sheed Ali, CA ANZ Qualified Auditor

New Zealand's financial reporting framework can seem complex, but understanding which standards apply to your organisation is essential for compliance. This guide breaks down the NZ reporting framework in plain language — covering the tier system, key standards, and how to determine which framework applies to you.

Who Sets the Standards?

The External Reporting Board (XRB) is New Zealand's independent crown entity responsible for issuing financial reporting standards. The XRB sets the accounting and assurance standards that govern how financial statements are prepared and audited in New Zealand.

The XRB establishes two main frameworks:

  • NZ IFRS — For profit-oriented entities (businesses)
  • PBE Standards — For public benefit entities (charities, NFPs, government)

The Multi-Tier Reporting Framework

New Zealand uses a tiered reporting framework that scales reporting requirements based on entity size. This means smaller organisations face less complex reporting obligations, while larger entities must comply with full standards. The tiers differ depending on whether your entity is for-profit or a public benefit entity.

For-Profit Entity Tiers

For profit-oriented entities, the tiers are based on annual revenue and total assets:

Tier Criteria Standard
Tier 1 Revenue over $30M OR assets over $60M Full NZ IFRS
Tier 2 Revenue $2M–$30M AND assets under $60M NZ IFRS RDR (Reduced Disclosure Requirements)
Non-Tier Revenue under $2M Simple format reporting or cash-based accounts

Tier 1 entities must apply full NZ IFRS with all disclosures. Tier 2 entities apply the same recognition and measurement requirements but with reduced disclosure requirements (RDR), meaning fewer notes and disclosures in the financial statements.

Public Benefit Entity (PBE) Tiers

For charities, not-for-profits, and public sector entities, the tiers are based on annual operating expenditure:

Tier Annual Operating Expenditure Standard
Tier 1 Over $33 million Full PBE IPSAS
Tier 2 $5M–$33M PBE IPSAS RDR
Tier 3 Generally under $5M Simple format accrual-based accounts
Tier 4 Up to $140K operating payments Simple format cash-based accounts

Tiers 1 and 2 use PBE International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS), with Tier 2 applying reduced disclosures. Tiers 3 and 4 use simplified formats — Tier 3 is accrual-based and Tier 4 is cash-based — making reporting much simpler for smaller organisations.

Which Framework Applies to You?

To determine which framework applies to your organisation, ask yourself:

  • Is your entity for-profit or a public benefit entity? — This determines whether you use NZ IFRS or PBE standards
  • What is your annual revenue/expenditure? — This determines your tier
  • Are there any specific regulatory requirements? — Some entities have additional reporting obligations under specific legislation (e.g., Charities Act, Companies Act)
  • Does your constitution or trust deed specify a reporting framework? — Some governing documents mandate particular reporting levels

Key Standards You Should Know

For Profit-Oriented Entities

  • NZ IAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements
  • NZ IAS 7 — Statement of Cash Flows
  • NZ IAS 16 — Property, Plant and Equipment
  • NZ IAS 36 — Impairment of Assets
  • NZ IFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers
  • NZ IFRS 16 — Leases

For Public Benefit Entities

  • PBE IPSAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements
  • PBE IPSAS 17 — Property, Plant and Equipment
  • PBE IPSAS 23 — Revenue from Non-Exchange Transactions (Taxes and Transfers)
  • PBE IPSAS 41 — Financial Instruments

What About Audit Requirements?

Your reporting tier also affects your audit requirements. Generally:

  • Tier 1 & 2 charities — Audit required under the Charities Act (currently, expenditure over $5M)
  • Tier 3 charities — Audit or review required ($550K–$1.1M); audit required over $1.1M–$5M. Currently these are the thresholds.
  • Tier 4 charities — No audit/review required unless constitution mandates (under $550K)
  • Large companies — Audit required under the Financial Reporting Act
  • Incorporated societies — Audit requirements depend on size under the Incorporated Societies Act 2022

For more detail, read our guides on charity audit requirements and trust audit requirements.

Common Reporting Mistakes

  • Using the wrong framework (e.g., a charity using for-profit standards)
  • Applying the wrong tier (e.g., a Tier 2 entity applying Tier 4 simple format)
  • Inconsistent application of accounting policies year-on-year
  • Missing required disclosures
  • Not preparing a statement of cash flows when required
  • Inadequate notes to the financial statements

Key Takeaways

  • The XRB sets NZ's financial reporting standards
  • For-profit entities use NZ IFRS; public benefit entities use PBE standards
  • Reporting requirements scale with entity size (tiers)
  • Tier 1 & 2 require full standards; Tier 3 & 4 use simplified formats
  • Your reporting tier affects your audit requirements
  • Always check specific legislation and your constitution for additional requirements
