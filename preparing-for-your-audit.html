Preparing for Your Audit: A Practical Checklist

9 min read Published 2025 Sheed Ali, CA ANZ Qualified Auditor

The key to a smooth, efficient audit is good preparation. When your records are organised and your team is ready, the audit process becomes faster, less disruptive, and often less expensive. This guide walks you through everything you need to do to prepare for your audit — with a practical checklist you can follow step by step.

Why Preparation Matters

Good preparation benefits everyone involved in the audit process:

  • Faster completion — Well-organised records mean less time spent searching for documents
  • Lower cost — Fewer auditor hours means a lower fee
  • Less disruption — You can get back to your day job sooner
  • Better outcomes — Clean records lead to fewer findings and adjustments
  • Stronger relationship — A smooth process builds trust with your auditor

The Audit Preparation Checklist

Here's our practical checklist, organised by category. Work through these items before the auditor arrives:

1. Financial Statements & Records

  • ☐ Draft financial statements prepared (or finalised if possible)
  • ☐ Trial balance reconciled to the general ledger
  • ☐ Bank reconciliations completed for all accounts at year-end
  • ☐ Debtors and creditors ledgers reconciled to the general ledger
  • ☐ Fixed asset register up to date with additions and disposals
  • ☐ Stock/inventory count completed and valued (if applicable)
  • ☐ Statement of cash flows prepared
  • ☐ Notes to the financial statements drafted

2. Supporting Documentation

  • ☐ Bank statements for all accounts (full year)
  • ☐ Loan agreements and amortisation schedules
  • ☐ Major contracts and agreements
  • ☐ Lease agreements (property, equipment, vehicles)
  • ☐ Insurance policies (summary of coverage)
  • ☐ Board/committee meeting minutes for the year
  • ☐ Significant correspondence with lenders, lawyers, or regulators
  • ☐ Grant/funding agreements and acquittal reports (for NFPs/charities)

3. Compliance & Governance

  • ☐ Constitution/trust deed/rule book available
  • ☐ Previous year's audit report available
  • ☐ Tax returns filed (or draft prepared)
  • ☐ GST returns reconciled to the general ledger
  • ☐ PAYE returns reconciled to wages
  • ☐ Any regulatory filings up to date (Charities Services, Companies Office, etc.)
  • ☐ Related party transactions identified and documented

4. Internal Controls

  • ☐ Authorisation procedures documented (who approves what)
  • ☐ Segregation of duties reviewed (where possible)
  • ☐ Password and access controls reviewed
  • ☐ Any known control weaknesses documented for the auditor
  • ☐ Any fraud or error incidents disclosed to the auditor

5. People & Access

  • ☐ Key staff identified and available for auditor questions
  • ☐ Workspace allocated for the auditor (if on-site)
  • ☐ Access to accounting software arranged (Xero, MYOB, etc.)
  • ☐ Document sharing platform set up (if working remotely)
  • ☐ Board/treasurer available for sign-off discussions

For Charities & NFPs: Additional Items

If you're a registered charity or not-for-profit, also prepare:

  • Performance report / statement of service performance drafted
  • Grants received — documentation showing conditions and use of funds
  • Donation records and receipts
  • Volunteer time records (if valued in financial statements)
  • Charities Services annual return (draft)
  • Any funder-specific reporting requirements

Tips for a Smooth Audit

  • Start early — Begin preparation at least 4–6 weeks before the audit fieldwork
  • Communicate — Keep in touch with your auditor throughout the preparation phase
  • Be responsive — Quick responses to auditor queries keep the process moving
  • Ask questions — If you're unsure what's needed, ask — we're here to help
  • Don't hide issues — Disclose known problems early; it's always better to be upfront
  • Use cloud accounting — If you use Xero, MYOB, or similar, give the auditor access early

What Happens After Preparation?

Once you've completed the checklist, your auditor will:

  • Review the draft financial statements and supporting documentation
  • Perform audit procedures (testing, verification, enquiries)
  • Discuss any findings or adjustments with you
  • Issue the audit report and management letter
  • Walk you through the results before finalising

Key Takeaways

  • Good preparation saves time, money, and stress
  • Use the checklist above to ensure you're audit-ready
  • Organise documents by category for easy access
  • Start preparation 4–6 weeks before fieldwork
  • Communicate openly with your auditor throughout
  • Disclose any known issues early — transparency builds trust
