Why NFPs Need Specialist Audit

Understanding NFP Audit Requirements

Not-for-profit organisations in New Zealand face unique challenges when it comes to audit and assurance. Whether you're an incorporated society, a community group, or a membership organisation, your reporting obligations depend on your legal structure, size, and constitutional requirements.

The Incorporated Societies Act 2022 introduced significant changes for incorporated societies, including new reporting and audit requirements based on your society's size and revenue. Understanding these changes is crucial for ensuring compliance and maintaining your society's good standing.

At SA Audit, we understand that many NFPs operate with limited financial resources and volunteer-based governance. Our approach is proportionate — providing the right level of assurance without unnecessary complexity or cost.

NFP Reporting Requirements

Audit & Review Requirements for NFPs

Your reporting obligations depend on your NFP's legal structure, size, and constitutional requirements. Here's a summary of key requirements.

Most Common

Financial Audit

High Assurance

  • Required for larger NFPs exceeding statutory thresholds
  • Required if your constitution mandates an audit
  • Provides the highest level of assurance to stakeholders
  • Satisfies funder and regulator requirements

Review Engagement

Moderate Assurance

  • Often acceptable where a full audit isn't required by law
  • Lower cost than a full audit with meaningful assurance
  • May satisfy constitutional requirements where "audit" is not specified
  • Sufficient for many funders and stakeholders

Agreed-Upon Procedures

No Assurance

  • Targeted verification of specific financial data
  • Most cost-effective option for specific needs
  • Useful for grant acquittals and funder verification
  • Reports factual findings without an assurance opinion

Incorporated Societies Act 2022

Key Changes for Incorporated Societies

The Incorporated Societies Act 2022 introduced new reporting and audit requirements. Here's what your society needs to know.

1

Large Societies

Societies with annual operating payments exceeding $3 million must prepare financial statements in accordance with NZ accounting standards and have them audited by a qualified auditor.

Mandatory: Full audit by a qualified auditor.

2

Medium Societies

Societies with annual operating payments between $66,000 and $3 million must prepare simplified financial statements. Audit requirements depend on your constitution.

Check your constitution — may need audit or review.

3

Small Societies

Societies with annual operating payments under $66,000 have simplified reporting requirements. However, your constitution may still require an audit.

Audit may still be required by your constitution.

Constitution Check

Regardless of size, if your constitution requires an audit, you must have one. Reviewing your constitution is the first step in determining your audit obligations.
