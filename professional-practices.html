Professional Practice Audits

Why Professional Practices Need an Audit

Professional practices face unique audit requirements. Law firms must comply with the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act trust account regulations. Accounting practices may require peer reviews or quality reviews. Many professional partnerships need audited financial statements for partner distributions and lending requirements.

Beyond compliance, an independent audit provides assurance to partners, lenders, and professional regulators that your financial management meets the high standards expected of professional service firms.

Trust account audits — Required for law firms and some other professions under regulatory rules
Partnership audits — Independent verification for partner distributions and profit allocations
Lender requirements — Banks and financiers often require audited financial statements for practice lending
Regulatory compliance — Professional body requirements for quality assurance and financial reporting
Our Professional Practice Services

What Our Audits Cover

Financial Statement Audit

Independent audit of practice financial statements, providing assurance for partners, lenders, and professional regulators.

Trust Account Audit

Regulatory audits of solicitors' trust accounts under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act, ensuring compliance with NZLS requirements.

Partnership Reporting

Assurance over profit allocation, partner drawings, and capital accounts — ensuring fair and transparent partner distributions.

Agreed-Upon Procedures

Targeted procedures on specific areas — WIP valuation, fee recovery, practice profitability — providing factual findings without a full audit opinion.

Practice Valuation Support

Independent assurance over financial data used in practice valuations, succession planning, and partner buy-in or exit arrangements.

Practical Recommendations

Management insights on billing efficiency, WIP management, lock-up days, and financial controls specific to professional practices.
