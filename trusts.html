Trust Audit Requirements

Why Trusts Need an Audit

Trustees have a fiduciary duty to manage trust assets responsibly and account to beneficiaries. An independent audit provides assurance that the trust's financial statements are reliable, and that trust assets have been properly managed.

For charitable trusts, an audit may be required by the trust deed, by Charities Services, or by legislation. For Māori trusts, audit requirements are governed by Te Ture Whenua Māori Act and the Māori Trustee. For family trusts, an audit can provide transparency and protection for both trustees and beneficiaries.

Charitable trusts — Audit required by Charities Services or trust deed for registered charities
Māori trusts — Audit required under Te Ture Whenua Māori Act for certain entities
Family trusts — Audit provides transparency, protects trustees, and reassures beneficiaries
Trust deed requirements — Many trust deeds mandate annual audits regardless of size
Trust Types

Types of Trusts We Audit

Charitable Trusts

Trusts established for charitable purposes registered with Charities Services. We audit financial statements and ensure compliance with reporting obligations.

Family Trusts

Trusts holding family assets for asset protection and succession planning. Independent audits protect trustees and provide transparency to beneficiaries.

Māori Trusts

Trusts governed by Te Ture Whenua Māori Act, including Māori trust boards and whenua Māori trusts. We understand the unique reporting and cultural requirements.

Community Trusts

Trusts serving community purposes, including community development trusts and energy trusts. We audit for accountability to stakeholders and funding bodies.

Education Trusts

Trusts supporting educational institutions or scholarships. We provide independent assurance over fund management and grant distribution.

Discretionary Trusts

Trusts where trustees have discretion over distributions. Audits provide beneficiaries with confidence that trustee decisions are made appropriately and documented.
