Charity Audit Requirements in NZ: A Complete Guide

10 min read Published 2025 Sheed Ali, CA ANZ Qualified Auditor

Registered charities in New Zealand operate under specific reporting obligations set out in the Charities Act 2005. Understanding these requirements is essential for maintaining your registered charity status and demonstrating accountability to funders, donors, and the public.

The Charities Act 2005 & Reporting Obligations

The Charities Act 2005 requires all registered charities to file an Annual Return with Charities Services, including their financial statements and a performance report. The level of reporting — and whether an audit is required — depends on your charity's size, determined by its annual operating expenditure.

Charity Reporting Tiers

Currently, New Zealand charities are classified into four tiers based on annual operating expenditure. Your tier determines your reporting and audit requirements:

Tier Annual Operating Expenditure Audit/Review Requirement
Tier 1 Over $33 million Audit required
Tier 2 Over $5 million to $33 million Audit required
Tier 3 $550,000 to $5 million Audit or review required for $550,000 to $1.1m. Audit is required over $1.1m to $5m.
Tier 4 Under $550,000 No audit/review required (unless constitution mandates)

When is an Audit Required?

An audit is mandatory for:

  • Tier 1 charities (annual expenditure over $33M)
  • Tier 2 charities (annual expenditure over $5M to $33M)
  • Charities whose constitution or trust deed requires an audit
  • Charities with specific funder or grant requirements

For Tier 3 charities (expenditure $550K–$5M), either an audit or a review is required for expenditure between $550,000 and $1.1m, while an audit is required for expenditure over $1.1m to $5m. Currently, these are the thresholds — your choice between audit and review (where applicable) may depend on your constitution, funder expectations, or board preference.

Tier 4 charities (under $550K) are not required to have an audit or review, unless their constitution specifically mandates it. However, many smaller charities choose to have a review or audit voluntarily to demonstrate accountability.

The Performance Report

All registered charities must prepare a performance report as part of their annual financial statements. This includes:

  • Statement of Service Performance — describing what the charity set out to achieve and what it actually delivered
  • Statement of Financial Position — balance sheet showing assets, liabilities, and equity/funds
  • Statement of Financial Performance — income and expenditure for the year
  • Statement of Cash Flows — cash movements during the year
  • Statement of Changes in Equity/Funds — movements in accumulated funds
  • Notes to the Financial Statements — supporting disclosures and accounting policies

The auditor reviews the performance report as part of the audit to ensure it meets NZ accounting standards and Charities Services requirements.

Filing Deadlines

Registered charities must file their Annual Return with Charities Services within 6 months of their balance date. This includes the completed Annual Return form, financial statements, and the auditor's report (if an audit or review was required).

Missing filing deadlines can result in your charity being flagged as non-compliant, which may affect your registered status and your ability to receive grants and donations.

Choosing a Qualified Auditor

Under the Charities Act, your audit must be conducted by a qualified auditor — a person who is a member of Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand (CA ANZ) holding a Certificate of Public Practice, or an equivalent professional body. Key Takeaways

  • Currently: Tier 1 & 2 charities must have an audit (expenditure over $5M)
  • Tier 3 charities need an audit or review ($550K–$1.1M), or audit over $1.1M–$5M
  • Tier 4 charities don't require an audit unless their constitution mandates it (under $550K)
  • All charities must file Annual Returns within 6 months of balance date
  • Audits must be conducted by a CA ANZ Qualified Auditor
  • The performance report is a key part of charity reporting
