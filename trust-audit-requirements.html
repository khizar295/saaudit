Trust Audit Requirements in New Zealand

8 min read Published 2025 Sheed Ali, CA ANZ Qualified Auditor

Trusts play an important role in New Zealand's financial landscape — from protecting family wealth to managing Māori land assets and supporting charitable purposes. But when does a trust actually need an audit? The answer depends on the type of trust, its governing legislation, and the requirements set out in the trust deed.

Types of Trusts in New Zealand

Before understanding audit requirements, it's helpful to know the main types of trusts in NZ:

  • Family trusts — Established for asset protection, estate planning, and wealth transfer between generations
  • Charitable trusts — Established for charitable purposes, often registered with Charities Services
  • Māori trusts — Governed by Te Ture Whenua Māori Act, including Māori trust boards and whenua Māori trusts
  • Trading trusts — Trusts that carry on business activities through a corporate trustee
  • Discretionary trusts — Trustees have discretion over distributions to beneficiaries

When Does a Family Trust Need an Audit?

Family trusts are not generally required by law to have an audit. However, an audit may be needed when:

  • The trust deed specifically requires an annual audit
  • A beneficiary requests an audit and the trust deed provides for this
  • The trust is also a registered charity (Charities Act requirements apply)
  • The trust has bank lending and the lender requires audited financials
  • Trustees want to demonstrate sound management and protect themselves from liability

Even when not required, many trustees choose to have an audit or review to provide transparency to beneficiaries and demonstrate that they've properly discharged their fiduciary duties.

Charitable Trust Audit Requirements

If your trust is also a registered charity, the Charities Act 2005 requirements apply. This means:

  • Tier 1 & 2 trusts (expenditure over $5M) — audit required. Currently these are the thresholds.
  • Tier 3 trusts (expenditure $550K–$5M) — audit or review required for $550K–$1.1M; audit required over $1.1M–$5M. Currently these are the thresholds.
  • Tier 4 trusts (under $550K) — not required unless the trust deed mandates it

Read our full guide on charity audit requirements for more detail.

Māori Trust Audit Requirements

Māori trusts are governed by Te Ture Whenua Māori Act 1993 (the Māori Land Act). Audit requirements depend on the type of entity:

  • Māori trust boards — Required to have annual financial statements audited under the Māori Trust Boards Act 1955
  • Whenua Māori trusts — The Māori Land Court may require audits depending on the trust order
  • Ahuwhenua trusts — Annual accounts must be prepared and may need to be audited
  • Māori incorporation — Must have annual financial statements audited

We have experience auditing Māori organisations and understand the unique governance and cultural considerations that apply.

Trading Trust Audit Requirements

Trading trusts that carry on business activities may need an audit if:

  • The corporate trustee is a large company exceeding Financial Reporting Act thresholds
  • The trust deed requires an audit
  • Lenders require audited financial statements
  • External shareholders or partners require assurance

What Does a Trust Audit Involve?

A trust audit examines the trust's financial statements and operations to provide assurance that:

  • Trust assets are properly recorded and safeguarded
  • Distributions to beneficiaries are made in accordance with the trust deed
  • Trustee decisions are properly documented
  • Financial statements present a true and fair view of the trust's financial position
  • The trust is complying with relevant legislation and the trust deed

Choosing a Qualified Auditor for Your Trust

Trust audits must be conducted by a qualified auditor — a CA ANZ member holding a Certificate of Public Practice. Key Takeaways

  • Family trusts don't legally require an audit unless the trust deed mandates it
  • Charitable trusts must comply with Charities Act requirements based on expenditure tiers
  • Māori trusts have specific requirements under Te Ture Whenua Māori Act
  • Trading trusts may need audits if they exceed company size thresholds
  • Always check your trust deed for audit requirements
  • Trust audits must be conducted by a CAANZ Qualified Auditor
